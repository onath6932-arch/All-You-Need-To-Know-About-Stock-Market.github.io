MODULE 06

Cautionary Historical Case Studies

DEBT & ACCOUNTING FAILURE

Penn Central Transportation Co. (1970)

Penn Central was formed by merging major rail lines. Despite reporting record paper profits, the company masked operational losses through aggressive accounting tricks (e.g., capitalizing real estate revaluations as income) while accumulating vast short-term debt.

▪ The Flaw: Operational cash deficit masked by non-operating accounting gains.

▪ Outcome: Largest American bankruptcy filing in 1970 ($2.6B debt default).

▪ Graham Lesson: Always verify operating cash flows relative to short-term obligations.

EXCESSIVE LEVERAGE & JUNK BONDS

NVF Corp. Takeover of Sharon Steel (1969)

NVF Corporation, a tiny entity, acquired Sharon Steel—a business seven times its size—by issuing huge volumes of high-yield sub-investment-grade debentures ("junk bonds") and subordinated debt.

▪ The Flaw: Interest charges consumed all operational earnings, leaving no safety margin.

▪ Outcome: Severe financial distress under economic slowdowns.

▪ Graham Lesson: High debt loads eliminate the Margin of Safety regardless of enterprise size.
